CHESS RPG — PURE-DATA AUTHORITATIVE CORE
=========================================

Purpose
-------

This document records the current rules architecture, where to find its
implementation, the constraints that must remain true, and the recommended
performance roadmap. It is a reference document, not a migration changelog.


Current Status
--------------

The pure-data core is the sole authoritative rules engine for live play and AI
simulation. It owns legal actions, movement, combat, abilities, reactions,
choices, delayed effects, turn resolution, battle completion, and deterministic
random state.

ChessBoardModel is now a live Godot facade. It owns signals, animation and
completion gates, position replacement, and ModelPiece projections required by
the controller and presentation. It does not contain an alternative rules
engine, and there is no legacy-authority fallback.

An unsupported or structurally invalid position is rejected at the core import
boundary instead of being interpreted by a second implementation.


Where the Rules Live
--------------------

The authoritative implementation is under:

    scripts/core/pure/

Start with these files:

* classic_chess_core.gd
  The main rules coordinator. It imports and exports positions, enumerates legal
  actions, defines ordinary Pawn/Knight/Bishop/Rook/Queen/King movement, handles
  castling, en passant and promotion, resolves submitted actions and choices,
  applies damage and destruction, orders reactions, advances turns, and emits
  semantic events.

* chess_core_state.gd
  The complete mutable simulation state: pieces, occupancy indexes, effects,
  autonomous entities, turn state, pending choices, reactions, IDs, and RNG.

* chess_core_piece.gd
  Pure piece data used by the rules core. These are not Nodes and have no visual
  behavior.

* chess_core_action.gd, chess_core_choice.gd, chess_core_reaction.gd,
  chess_core_event.gd, and chess_core_step_result.gd
  The typed inputs and outputs of a rules transition.

* chess_core_king_rules.gd
  The registry connecting king type IDs to their special-rule modules.

King-specific rules are divided into:

* arakne_core_rules.gd — Skitter paths and Spike Burst.
* necromancer_core_rules.gd — Bone Pawn summoning; shared core resolution owns
  Raise Dead and Bone Pawn lifecycle.
* minotaur_core_rules.gd — Charge and Retaliating Rage policy data.
* quarterly_initiative_core_rules.gd — Efficiency, Performance Cycle, and
  movement grants.
* cannon_core_rules.gd — Arc Mortar targeting and area rules.
* rhetorician_core_rules.gd — attack prevention, compelled movement, Lunacy,
  and Socratic behavior.
* wraith_core_rules.gd — summon, replacement, and pursuit policy data; shared
  core resolution owns capture-square Cursed Marks and projectile-only chained
  movement through marks.
* archer_core_rules.gd — all Archer variants, ranged attacks, Draw, Quiver,
  Charge, Volley, arrow effects, and Archer RNG behavior.

Some cross-mechanic resolution necessarily remains in classic_chess_core.gd.
For example, destruction can queue Necromancer or Minotaur reactions, and turn
entry can resolve Mortars, Wraiths, cooldowns, or Socratic actions. The small
king modules describe their local geometry and effects; the coordinator composes
those effects through one shared action lifecycle.


Live and AI Integration
-----------------------

* scripts/core/classic_core_live_bridge.gd
  Submits live commands to the core and translates semantic events into existing
  Godot signals and completion gates. Presentation may delay an animation, but
  it cannot alter the already-resolved outcome.

* scripts/core/chess_model.gd
  The live facade and ModelPiece projection. Its query and submit methods
  delegate to the core-backed bridge.

* scripts/pieces/
  ModelPiece presentation identities and authored metadata. These files may
  provide names, textures, cooldown labels, or custom projected state, but are
  not the authoritative source of legal movement, damage, or abilities.

* scripts/ai/dupre_classic_core_backend.gd
  Dupre's direct pure-data simulation adapter. It avoids temporary scene-tree
  models while preserving the same authoritative transitions as live play.

* scripts/state/chess_position*.gd
  The stable serialized position boundary used by saves, records, tests, and
  core import/export.


Supported Roster and State
--------------------------

The core currently supports ordinary chess pieces, Bone Pawns, Classic King,
Arakne, Necromancer, Minotaur, Quarterly Initiative King, Cannon, Rhetorician,
Wraith, and all current Archer variants.

It also represents granted movement types, cooldown state, Cursed Marks,
Archer arrows, Arc Mortars, Wraith entities, reactions, secondary choices,
forced passes, promotions, Lunacy, Socratic turns, and the RNG streams used by
current mechanics.


Measured Baseline
-----------------

The first fixed-work Dupre benchmark measured approximately 2.08x the simulation
throughput of temporary ChessBoardModel simulation and approximately 2.66x as
much tactical work within the same 1.2-second search budget.

A later 1,959-action mixed-king soak measured average action resolution at about
1.81 ms in the direct core versus 4.61 ms through the Node-based facade, about a
2.55x difference. Legal-action generation measured about 1.44 ms versus 1.84 ms,
about a 1.28x difference. These are development baselines, not permanent
guarantees; rerun comparable benchmarks after meaningful rules or search work.

The architectural gain is equally important: Dupre no longer constructs,
connects, and disposes a temporary graph of ChessBoardModel and ModelPiece Nodes
for every speculative branch.


Non-Negotiable Constraints
--------------------------

* Live play and AI simulation use the same rules.
* The core remains independent of SceneTree, animation timing, signals, and
  presentation completion gates.
* Native action identity is preserved, including path-dependent actions.
* Random state is explicit, isolated, serializable, and reproducible.
* Reactions, summons, delayed effects, hazards, and secondary choices remain
  explicit core state rather than AI approximations.
* Presentation consumes outcomes; it does not decide them.
* New king mechanics are implemented in the core first and projected through
  the facade, never duplicated in ModelPiece subclasses.
* Performance work must preserve deterministic replay and conformance tests.


Recommended Next Steps
----------------------

Completed foundation: Dupre now records isolated pure-core state-copy and
resolution costs per search, including action-kind and king-type buckets, silent
event counts, and maximum copy duration. Its existing search profile continues
to report legal generation, evaluation, completed replies, deadline fallbacks,
and uninterrupted intervals. These metrics are intentionally non-authoritative
and never enter serialized game state.

Dupre also has a shared, phase-tapered endgame evaluator used by both root policy
and pure-core leaf evaluation. It recognizes passed-pawn progress and urgency,
king activity, rook support behind passers, and whether simplification helps the
materially leading side. It does not alter rules or add search depth, and its
influence is zero in material-rich middlegames.

The endgame policy has internal master and technique A/B switches, enabled by
default, and a fixed-seed gameplay soak at
tests/headless/dupre_endgame_soak.tscn. The default gate runs short race,
conversion, Classic, Arakne, and Necromancer endgames with the established
evaluator and the new technique layer. Pass `-- --endgame-profile=soak` for
three seeds and longer games, or `-- --endgame-fixture=<name>` to print a
detailed action trace for one fixture. The short gate treats outcomes as
behavioral evidence rather than an exact strength rating because budget
fallbacks remain explicitly unverified.

The first gate held aggregate material equal, improved aggregate endgame score
slightly, and reduced immediate reversals from two to zero. It also showed that
endgame root bonuses can steer timeout fallback before tactical verification is
complete. Root influence is therefore intentionally only twenty percent of the
shared evaluator's score; completed tactical leaf evaluation retains the full
signal.

Dupre's timeout fallback now keeps categorical evidence for every prepared root:
terminal loss, known severe loss, incomplete forcing-reply coverage, or clear
forcing coverage. Partial minimax scores remain optimistic bounds and are never
compared as exact results. When no root completes, Dupre first chooses the least
risky category, rejects unsettled speculative abilities where possible, then
uses mandatory-purpose priority and policy score as deterministic tie-breakers.
The search profile and per-candidate diagnostics expose the selected risk,
forcing coverage, known loss, and classification cost.

Dupre also keeps a bounded history of settled authoritative positions. Its
signature retains every rule-relevant piece, ability, hazard, turn, and battle
field while ignoring absolute round number and RNG bookkeeping; last-move state
is retained only when it can still enable en passant. Returning to a prior state
penalizes only quiet root choices, so wins, captures, defenses, and forcing king
attacks are never suppressed merely because a position occurred before. History
is recorded after authoritative reactions settle and resets with the AI match
session.

The optional endgame technique layer adds side-to-move promotion clocks,
initial-double-move awareness, conservative blocker detection, King escort and
interception pressure, and purposeful rook support/blockade relationships. It
does not implement orthodox check or opposition: threatened squares remain
legal under Chess RPG's damage rules. Race clocks are positional estimates only;
authoritative tactical simulation still decides whether accepting an attack is
safe, losing, or lethal.

1. Profile full representative matches and optimize measured hotspots.
   The next useful sample is not another synthetic architecture migration: run
   fixed-seed Classic and mixed-king matches, compare endgame and middlegame
   profiles, and identify whether state copying, resolution, legal generation,
   tactical maps, or policy projection now dominates actual turns.

2. Optimize the GDScript core before porting it.
   Reuse per-state legal actions and tactical summaries, avoid unnecessary
   copies of immutable/transient data, reduce hot Dictionary and Variant use,
   avoid repeated board scans, and suppress semantic-event construction during
   silent simulations when search does not consume those events.

3. Spend recovered time on Dupre's judgment.
   Extend the new endgame foundation only from gameplay evidence: promotion
   races, opposition, rook endgames, repetition awareness, and king-specific
   endgame ability timing are the likely next candidates. Improve forcing-reply
   ordering and conservative quiescence before blindly increasing depth.

   Timeout-safe tactical fallback, bounded repetition awareness, promotion-race
   tempo, King escort/interception, and basic rook conversion technique are now
   in place. Extend them only from observed games, with fixtures which permit
   several sensible moves rather than demanding one brittle exact action.

4. Prototype bounded worker search.
   Copy one settled position on the main thread, use isolated state and RNG per
   worker, return data only, cancel on position-revision changes, and retain
   conservative desktop/mobile worker limits. No worker may touch Nodes.

5. Run one narrow native experiment after profiling.
   Port only a stable measured hotspot—such as state copying, attack-map
   construction, or legal movement generation—to a GDExtension. Compare fixed
   work against optimized GDScript before considering a broader native port.

Do not rewrite the entire core in C++ merely because native code may be faster.
The GDScript core is currently easier to evolve as king mechanics change. Native
code should earn its maintenance cost through a focused, reproducible benchmark.


Validation Pointers
-------------------

The principal characterization and conformance suites are under tests/headless/.
Useful entry points include classic_core_characterization.tscn,
classic_core_live_authority_characterization.tscn,
classic_core_live_presentation_characterization.tscn,
cross_king_core_soak.tscn, and the king-specific core suites.

The cross-king soak compares legal action and path identity, choices, serialized
state, RNG, lifecycle settlement, and mixed-mechanic behavior. Any native,
threaded, or state-copy optimization must continue to pass these gates.
