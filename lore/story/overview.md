# Story overview

Status: Working

## Confirmed goals

- The story supports multiple distinct endings.
- Party members, relationships, and mutable locations are part of its
  narrative structure.
- The [starting village](../locations/starting-village.md) is the current
  production focus.

The player begins as an unimportant person with an ambiguous background. They
travel and explore, collect king pieces, grow stronger at chess, raise their
Elo rating and tournament rank, and ultimately pursue the title of Champion.

The game contains two connected tracks. The first is visible and sufficient to
complete the game. The second is discovered through curiosity and choice.

## First playable sequence

After character creation, the player collapses outside the starting village
tavern in the rain and is sheltered by the tavernkeeper. On coming downstairs
the next day, they face their first thematic choice: protect a boy from a
powerful bully at personal risk, remain silent, or join the cruelty.

The branch changes the tavernkeeper, boy, brute, and onlookers' relationships
with the player. The story then reconverges through the professor, an ordinary
game, and the player's first king piece. See the
[opening tavern challenge](../quests/opening-tavern-challenge.md).

Afterward, the game opens into village exploration rather than immediately
starting another mandatory sequence. Across several parts of the day/night
cycle, the player can meet residents, explore the boundaries, and undertake
microquests. The village's [bridge decision](../quests/bridge-decision.md) begins
only after that breathing room.

## Champion track: the surface

The surface journey aims for the clarity and momentum of a classic
monster-collecting RPG:

```text
Local games and tournaments
    -> Rank and rating gains
        -> Fame and sponsors
            -> E4 Grand Finals
                -> Champion
```

The player travels between communities, becomes better at chess, collects king
pieces, explores dungeons, and may earn badge-like credentials. The exact rank,
badge, and qualification structure remains open.

Players can help people and explore optional stories along the way, but they do
not have to uncover the hidden conspiracy. Becoming Champion can end the story
on a complete, warm, and celebratory note.

## Occult track: the subterranean

Players with a nose for inconsistencies can follow threads beneath the public
journey:

```text
Corruption
    -> Culture wars
        -> Ritual energy leeching
            -> The ancient necromancer organization
```

Those threads reveal collusion involving the tournament organization, monarchy,
science and finance sectors, casinos, E4, and an ancient organization of
necromancers. The organization seeks to renew an occult pact with a malevolent
cosmic chess entity and is willing to sacrifice ordinary people to become
master of the world's board.

The public track can grant access, fame, relationships, and proximity that help
the player investigate. Exploration and optional choices move the player down
into the occult track; discoveries there can in turn change how they understand
or navigate the Championship.

## Occult endgame

Once deeply involved, the player can try to stop the ritual or hijack it and
claim its power, pursuing something like a Dark Lord ending. Seizing the ritual
may not work as cleanly as the player hopes.

Either course can culminate in a martial chess confrontation in which each
piece represents a platoon of combatants. The necromancers' armies face whatever
forces the player has been able to muster: friends, companions, communities and
defenders of the realm, or followers and subjects brought under the player's
control by any effective means.

The final force is therefore an accounting of the player's journey. Earlier
relationships and location outcomes determine not only who is available, but
why they stand with the player.

## High-level narrative spine

Fill this in at the level of pressure and choice before adding a detailed
sequence of events.

| Movement | Surface journey | Optional subterranean journey |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opening | An unknown player is sheltered in the starting village, makes a consequential first choice, receives a king, and begins to travel. | Small inconsistencies can be noticed but need not be pursued. |
| Ascent | The player acquires kings, rank, skill, fame, and access. | Separate cases begin to suggest institutional corruption and coordination. |
| Convergence | The player approaches the highest levels of the tournament system. | The ancient organization and ritual design become legible. |
| Resolution | The player may become Champion and end their journey there. | The player attempts to stop or seize the ritual and musters forces for the final confrontation. |

The movements do not need equal length and do not imply a rigid act structure.

## What must be known to build the village

- The player's immediate identity, desire, and reason for being there
- What the village teaches the player to care about
- The local conflict and the larger theme it demonstrates in miniature
- The important NPCs' conflicting wants
- What can change in the village
- The hook that points beyond it

## What can wait

- The complete history of the world
- A final name and biography for every distant antagonist
- Exact quest order in later regions
- Every companion arc
- Exact ending permutations

Later facts can be written around a strong opening. If a future idea requires a
change, revise the village deliberately rather than keeping it abstract forever.

## Open questions

- What brings the otherwise ambiguous player to the starting village?
- What does the player believe they want at the beginning, and what deeper need
  eventually complicates it?
- What exactly constitutes rating, rank, badges or qualifications, and what does
  the title of Champion grant?
- How much can the two tracks affect each other before the occult endgame?
- What discovery marks the point at which the player is truly involved rather
  than merely curious?
- Can an informed player refuse both stopping and seizing the ritual, and what
  would that refusal mean?
- Which early decision could echo naturally in the final resolution?
