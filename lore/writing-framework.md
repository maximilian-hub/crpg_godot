# Writing framework

Status: Working

## Big-to-small process

Writing development moves from the largest shared foundations toward authored
scenes:

```text
World foundations
    -> Core themes and questions
        -> Overarching player journey
            -> Major areas and plotlines
                -> Locations, factions, and characters
                    -> Quests, choices, and consequences
                        -> Scenes and dialogue
```

Later work can expose a weakness in an earlier layer, so iteration is expected.
The point is not to make the process rigid. It is to prevent a character, quest,
or location from being fully authored before the larger story knows what that
content is for.

## Thematic cascade

Each layer translates the same concerns into a more concrete form:

| Layer | The question it must answer |
| --- | --- |
| World | What conditions make the core question unavoidable? |
| Theme | What human question organizes the story, and what answers are possible? |
| Player journey | How does the player encounter, evade, and eventually answer it? |
| Major area | What large-scale variation or institution puts it under pressure? |
| Location conflict | Who is being used as a pawn here, and what competing visions of a good life collide? |
| Character | What answer does this person live, why, and what does it cost them? |
| Quest | What situation forces those answers into action? |
| Choice | What answer does the player enact, knowingly or otherwise? |
| Consequence | How does the world remember and interpret that action? |

This is an organizing principle, not a requirement that every conversation
explicitly discuss the theme.

## Theme growth

The [core theme](themes/core.md) and cultivation–domination spectrum are the
starting foundation, not a claim that the story has only one idea. Other major
themes may emerge during development. When one becomes structurally important,
give it:

- A central question
- A spectrum or set of competing answers
- Characters who embody meaningfully different positions
- Locations or quests that test variations of it
- A documented relationship to the core theme

Characters are multidimensional. A position on the primary spectrum does not
determine their positions on freedom, duty, compassion, tradition, faith,
knowledge, ambition, or future thematic axes. Two characters can occupy a
similar part of one spectrum and still disagree about nearly everything else.

## Development order for the demo

The demo should proceed through these gates:

1. Establish the world premise, central question, primary spectrum, and
   high-level player journey.
2. Define the starting village's variation of the central question.
3. Define the local conflict and its possible consequences.
4. Create the people whose needs and beliefs make that conflict human.
5. Break the conflict into quests, discoveries, games, and choices.
6. Write scenes and dialogue only after their dramatic purpose is known.

Gate 1 now has a working answer. The active work begins at Gate 2.
