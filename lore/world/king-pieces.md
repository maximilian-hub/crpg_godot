# King pieces

Status: Working

## Nature

King pieces are magical objects or artifacts with a kind of soul or life. Before
someone connects with one, a king is inert stone: neither white nor black and
unable to participate fully in a game.

Kings can appear as magical instantiations of the energy, activity, emotional
landscape, or human history concentrated in a place or person. Their emergence
can be strange but locally meaningful, much as a supernatural creature might
arise from a particular habitat, industry, death, or accumulation of feeling.

Examples currently establishing the range include:

| Circumstance | Possible manifestation |
| --- | --- |
| Mushrooms growing inside a rotting forest log | A Mushroom King hidden among them |
| A field plowed and tended by generations of farmers | A Farmer King |
| A corporate boardroom saturated with deals and abstract organizational discussion | A Quarterly Initiative King appearing beneath the table, perhaps found by a janitor |
| The crypt of an ancient and powerful necromancer after their final passing | A Necromancer King |
| The death of a humble truck driver | A Trucker King found by the driver's widow |

A king does not need to represent a socially grand subject. Humble labor and
ordinary lives can leave enough meaning to instantiate one just as surely as
power, death, or corporate ritual.

## Emotional landscape

Each kind of king embodies a particular emotional tone or landscape. Connecting
requires a player to summon emotions that resonate with it.

For example, the Necromancer King answers to deep greed, curiosity, and lust for
power. This does not yet establish whether every compatible player experiences
those emotions identically, whether a king judges sincerity, or how sustained
the emotional state must be.

## Connection and activation

Once resonance is achieved, the player reaches their life essence—their Chess
Power—into the piece. Chess Power manifests as pure white or black checkerboard
lightning or beams.

The connection:

- Runs in both directions between player and king
- Animates the formerly inert stone
- Brings the king into the color of the army the player is using
- Allows the player to move the king without touching it directly
- Gives the player access to the king's unusual powers
- Makes the connected player vulnerable through the piece

The existing in-game king activation ritual depicts part of this process.

## Defeat, resignation, and harm

When an opponent threatens a king with a final blow, its player may offer a
resigning handshake. The opponent is not compelled to accept. They may reject
the resignation and strike the king anyway.

Because the king is connected to its player, defeat can carry more than the
ordinary psychological pain of losing. Depending on the winner's intent, the
final strike can physically hurt the defeated player and may hurt them
seriously.

This makes restraint part of chess etiquette and makes a hostile game a genuine
act of bodily risk. The exact laws, customs, healing, and limits surrounding
harmful final blows are not yet established.

## Thematic implications

Kings literalize the relationship between people and the worlds they create.
Places, kinds of work, institutions, deaths, and shared emotional environments
can produce living power. Connecting lets a person act through that power, but
also exposes their life to it.

This supports the [core theme](../themes/core.md) without predetermining a single
interpretation: a player may cultivate a relationship with a king, use it as a
tool of domination, or discover that the apparent tool has needs and influence
of its own.

## Open questions

- Are individual king artifacts unique, or can the same king archetype manifest
  more than once?
- Do kings choose players, resist connection, or develop preferences?
- How aware are kings while inert or connected?
- Can a person connect with several kings, and can connections persist outside
  a game?
- What exactly determines whether a final blow causes psychological discomfort,
  physical injury, or death?
- Can harmful intent be concealed, accidental, resisted, or proven afterward?
- What laws or customs govern refusal of a resigning handshake?
- How does a player recover after Chess Power is damaged?
- What kind of king, if any, can someone use before receiving their first
  special king piece?
- How does the conspiracy's ritual energy leeching relate to ordinary Chess
  Power and king connections?
