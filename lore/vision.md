# Creative vision

Status: Working

## Core premise

The existing project premise is a role-playing game in a world where people
settle conflict through chess, and where every king can change the rules of the
game. This is stated in the project [README](../README.txt).

The player begins as an unimportant person with an intentionally ambiguous
background, travels the world, becomes stronger at chess, acquires king pieces,
raises their Elo rating, and rises through the tournament system toward the
Championship.

The game's organizing thematic question and primary spectrum are defined in
the [core theme](themes/core.md).

## Confirmed design goals

The intended narrative complexity sits somewhere between *Pokemon Crystal* and
*Dragon Age: Origins*: legible and adventurous rather than encyclopedic, but
with enough reactivity for choices and relationships to matter.

The game is intended to include:

- Multiple meaningfully distinct endings
- Recruitable party members
- Places that can improve, decline, or be ruined through player action
- Relationships that can become warm, hostile, trusting, resentful, or
  otherwise meaningfully different
- Quest choices whose consequences remain visible after the quest ends

## Two depths of story

The surface game can play like a classic monster-collecting adventure: travel
from place to place, explore, acquire kings, improve at chess, enter tournaments,
gain rank and fame, and eventually become Champion. Helping people and pursuing
side stories may enrich that journey without being required to uncover a darker
main plot. Reaching the Championship can be a complete, cute, and satisfying
ending.

Attentive or inquisitive players can discover a second story beneath that
journey. Following its threads reveals institutional corruption, collusion, and
an occult conspiracy that radically increases the stakes. The
[story overview](story/overview.md) owns the relationship between these tracks.

Both modes are authentic versions of the game. The surface is not merely bait
or a false story, and players who do not uncover the occult track have not
failed to play correctly.

## Narrative promises

These are the promises the writing should try to keep even before the main plot
is fully outlined:

- Opponents feel like people with histories, habits, and incomplete knowledge,
  not chess engines wearing portraits.
- A victory on the board does not automatically answer what the right thing was
  to do.
- The player changes communities and relationships, not only inventory and
  combat strength.
- Important choices are remembered by the world.
- Special kings are discoveries with cultural and personal meaning, not only
  alternate move sets. Their current nature is defined in
  [king pieces](world/king-pieces.md).
- Warmth, humor, ordinary work, and local community remain sincere even though
  the setting contains systemic exploitation and cosmic horror.
- Curiosity is rewarded with deeper context, not treated as a mandatory tax on
  players who primarily want the chess adventure.

## Open questions

- Is the overall tone hopeful, tragicomic, eerie, earnest, or a deliberate mix?
- Is chess primarily law, ritual, sport, magic, religion, violence by proxy, or
  several of these depending on culture?
- What does losing a formal game entitle the winner to demand?
- What privileges, duties, or dangers come with tournament rank and the title
  of Champion?
- What content would violate the desired tone even if it were individually
  interesting?
