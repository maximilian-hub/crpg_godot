# World

Status: Working

## Confirmed design premise

- People settle conflict through chess.
- Different kings change the rules of chess.
- [King pieces](world/king-pieces.md) are living magical artifacts that can
  manifest from the accumulated energy and meaning of places, people, work, and
  death.

The exact reach, enforcement, and origin of these practices are not yet
documented.

## Current direction with lore implications

The [AI design north star](../AI_DESIGN_NORTH_STAR.txt) treats inhabitants as
chess players rather than omniscient engines:

- Most people specialize in one king or a small collection.
- A person can be unfamiliar with an obscure king and learn from seeing it.
- Familiarity belongs to the individual; the player's discovery record and an
  NPC's knowledge are separate things.
- Ordinary people such as farmers, butchers, and goblins can be legitimate
  opponents without being masters of the entire known game.

This supports a world where chess knowledge has geography, class, fashion,
teachers, local traditions, and personal history.

## Institutional landscape

The high-level story currently involves these centers of power:

- The Kingdom's monarchy
- The tournament organization through which players pursue rank and the
  Championship
- Scientific and financial sectors
- Casinos
- [E4](factions/e4.md), a megacorporation connected to the upper reaches of the
  public tournament journey, land acquisition, and infrastructure

Their exact structures, names, jurisdictions, and relationships remain open.
What matters at this stage is that the player's apparently straightforward rise
through competitive chess can bring them into contact with increasingly
powerful institutions.

## Hidden order

Behind parts of those institutions is an ancient organization of literal
necromancers, currently described out of world as the “chess illuminati.” Its
final name, history, membership, and degree of control remain to be developed.

The organization previously attempted, and now seeks to complete, an occult
ritual pact with a malevolent cosmic chess entity. Its members seek to become
masters of the world's board. They are willing to expend ordinary people across
the Kingdom to secure their own hegemony and fulfill their beliefs.

Their influence operates through corruption and collusion among the tournament
organization, monarchy, science, finance, casinos, and E4. It should be
discoverable through accumulated threads rather than common public knowledge.
The [story overview](story/overview.md) owns how the player encounters this plot.

## Ordinary life

The conspiracy does not make every place secretly miserable or every
institutional participant consciously evil. Communities such as the
[starting village](locations/starting-village.md) can contain warmth, meaningful
work, mutual reliance, and genuine good lives. The core question matters because
there is something worth preserving beyond the institutions' board.

## Existing in-world claims

[Hood](characters/hood.md) tells the player that old forest statues are said to
depict a king and queen who abandoned their kingdom, power, and wealth to live
quietly in the woods. The nearby inspections show weathered statues of a man
and woman and a smaller figure that was intentionally destroyed.

This is a legend told by a guarded stranger, not an objective account. The
rulers' identities, the destroyed figure, and Hood's relationship to them are
open.

## World questions

These questions matter more than dates, dynasties, or a complete map:

- Who recognizes a chess result as binding, and what happens when someone
  refuses it?
- What can be staked or compelled in a game?
- Can anyone use any king, or do kings choose, require, alter, or endanger their
  players?
- How are kings found, inherited, made, traded, stolen, or lost?
- How common is ordinary violence outside formal play?
- How do wealth and political power affect access to training and rare kings?
- What does an average villager believe about the world's origin and the nature
  of kings?
- Which parts of the world reject or reinterpret the dominant chess customs?

The starting village only needs local answers. Other cultures may contradict
its assumptions later.
