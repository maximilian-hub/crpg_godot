# Starting village

Status: Working

## Core identity

The starting village is a warm, modest rural community on a long mesa. It is
backwoods but unusually worldly, unglamorous but economically buoyed by
outsiders, and accustomed to welcoming people who will soon move on.

Its square is mostly well-stamped dirt rather than paved stone. It is mostly
human and does not have the wealth or monumental architecture of the Kingdom's
main city. Simple living here is generally good rather than desperate. The
place should feel inhabited and cared for, not like a collection of RPG service
vendors.

*Dragon's Dogma's* Cassardis is a tonal reference for a small community with
warmth, identity, and an ordinary life worth valuing. It is not a literal model
for the village's culture or layout.

## Place in the story

This is the player's first real community and the setting of the demo. It should
establish all of the following without needing to explain the whole world:

- Ordinary people play chess and have personal relationships to it.
- The Championship creates travel, aspiration, money, and cultural exchange.
- A modest life outside the centers of power can be genuinely worthwhile.
- Communities contain their own compromises and disagreements before the player
  arrives.
- Curiosity may reveal a deeper context, but the local story remains coherent
  for a player following only the surface adventure.

The village's local conflict and the player's immediate reason for becoming
involved remain open.

## First playable sequence

After character creation, the player enters from the right at night in the rain
and collapses outside the tavern. The tavernkeeper finds them and gives them a
room. Late the following day, the player comes downstairs into the
[opening tavern challenge](../quests/opening-tavern-challenge.md).

That confrontation is the first personal-scale test of the central question.
It is not yet the village-scale conflict that organizes the rest of the demo.

## Geography

- The village sits on a long mesa between two canyons.
- A deep crevice divides part of the mesa near the settlement.
- A bridge crosses the crevice and is part of the village's physical identity.
- The storm associated with the player's arrival leaves the bridge facing a
  difficult and dangerous repair.
- The through-road makes the settlement a natural stopping point between a
  large nearby region and the Kingdom's main city.
- The village has a local water source, but it is not navigable and does not
  support waterborne travel.
- The surrounding cliffs support wildflowers and unusual cliffside beekeeping.

The exact map, the source and reliability of the water, and the relationship
between the bridge and the main road are not yet defined.

## Economy and daily life

### Traveler economy

Traffic increases around the beginning and end of tournament season. Spectators,
workers, and chess players with Championship ambitions pass through. The
village is therefore a little more economically active and cosmopolitan than
its size and remoteness would otherwise suggest.

The tavern is its most developed and important establishment. It benefits
considerably from transient business and makes its owning family relatively
prosperous by local standards.

A handful of residents participate in a rustic tourist economy by selling
trinkets to travelers. Some make chess pieces from beeswax.

### Local production

- The mesa does not support major agriculture.
- Families may maintain small household gardens for their own use.
- A distinctive type of plum tree grows only here, giving the village a unique
  local product.
- Beekeepers lower themselves by rope along the cliff face to install and tend
  hives near wildflowers growing from the rock. The bees produce a special
  cliff-flower honey.
- A nearby “bone quarry” contains old, strange, unusually large bones. Villagers
  occasionally incorporate a bone into local architecture. Someone may offer
  tours of the quarry to passersby.

The village's broader food supply is still unresolved. It may rely partly on
trade, gathering, or production outside the compact settlement.

## Local chess character

Constant travelers expose the village to more playing styles, rumors, and
competitive ambitions than a similarly small settlement would normally see.
The exact place of formal tournaments, local ranking, and casual play in daily
life remains to be developed.

One established local oddity is chess against the cliff bees. The bees hop onto
the board and act as the pieces themselves. The practical rules, origin of the
custom, and degree of bee intelligence are not yet defined.

Chess can cause real bodily harm through a player's connection to a
[king piece](../world/king-pieces.md). A hostile player may refuse a resigning
handshake and strike the connected king with harmful intent. The village's
customs and ability to restrain such players remain open.

## Canyon camp

A nearby group lives in a canyon camp. They look and behave enough like bandits
that outsiders would readily call them bandits, and they formerly held up
travelers on the road. The village began trading with them, and the relationship
settled into an informal coexistence. They now mostly want the freedom to live
as they please.

The camp values bones from the quarry. It may provide plant food or medicine
that grows in the wilderness, but its farming and gathering practices are still
tentative. See the [canyon camp](canyon-camp.md).

## Institutional presence

The village remains peripheral to the corporate and occult system as a community,
but [E4](../factions/e4.md) has taken interest in its transportation corridor.
The company's Land Acquisition division previously offered to acquire the road
and necessary land or rights for an interstate. The tavernkeeper's father
opposed the proposal.

The damaged bridge makes E4's promise of professional infrastructure and a
sizable purchase payment newly tempting. E4 need not care about the village's
internal life to exert enormous influence over it.

Occult influence should remain subtle. The land deal works as ordinary corporate
power even if the employees involved know nothing about the hidden conspiracy.

## Working village conflict

The [bridge decision](../quests/bridge-decision.md) is the working village-scale
storyline. The community must decide whether to accept E4's acquisition offer or
attempt a dangerous repair under local control.

This is the first serious problem since the tavernkeeper's father became too ill
to lead. Until now, the remaining elders and ordinary village arrangements have
functioned well enough without him.

## Thematic foundation

The village is not primarily an example of deprivation. It establishes a small
but real good life that people have cultivated together and that larger systems
might overlook, exploit, absorb, or endanger.

Its existing relationships already contain useful thematic material:

- The traveler economy brings opportunity without yet fully defining the
  village around outside power.
- The former bandits and villagers replaced recurring predation with trade,
  without requiring the camp to become respectable or surrender its autonomy.
- Cliff beekeeping turns dangerous collective knowledge and labor into a local
  good.
- The tavern converts transient ambition into durable community prosperity and
  hospitality.

These are foundations for the working variation of the
[core question](../themes/core.md): when shared safety requires dangerous
sacrifice, who should bear the risk, and how much control can a community trade
away to escape it?

The opening confrontation already establishes the question at an individual
scale: will the player risk being hurt for a child, protect themselves through
silence, or seek advantage through complicity? The wider village conflict
varies and deepens that question at a collective scale rather than merely
repeating another bullying scene.

## Local leadership

The tavernkeeper's father was not only the former proprietor but a leader among
the village elders and a highly respected voice in local affairs. His illness
created a latent informal power vacuum, but the village handled ordinary life
well enough that the gap did not feel like a crisis.

The bridge finally presents a problem large, dangerous, and divisive enough to
expose that absence. During the village deliberation, residents press to visit
him for guidance. He dies after an affectionate farewell, leaving the player as
the only person who clearly heard his final opposition to selling.

## Open questions

- What is the village called?
- Why does the player arrive, and what do they want before the local problem
  complicates that goal?
- Who governs, mediates disputes, and speaks for the community now that the
  tavernkeeper's father is ill?
- Which elders and residents support repair or sale, and why?
- How are chess challenges used locally, and who recognizes their results?
- What form does the water source take?
- How does the village feed itself beyond gardens, plums, and trade?
- How intelligent or magical are the chess-playing bees?
- What are the quarry bones, and how much do villagers actually know about them?
- How close and visible is the canyon camp, and what obligations exist on each
  side of the trade relationship?
- What exact rights and land would E4 acquire, and how would the interstate
  change local traffic and identity?
- Why is the player present for the father's final words on every opening branch?
- What makes helping E4 personally tempting to the player?
