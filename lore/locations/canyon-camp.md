# Canyon camp

Status: Seed

“Canyon camp” and “bandits” are development labels, not established in-world
names.

## Core identity

A rough, freedom-seeking community living in a canyon near the
[starting village](starting-village.md). Its members look and act enough like
bandits to alarm outsiders, but they have largely stopped preying on the local
road and settled into an informal trade relationship with the village.

## Established foundation

- The group formerly held up people traveling through the village's road.
- Trade with the village replaced or displaced that pattern of robbery.
- The camp now mostly keeps to itself and values the freedom to live on its own
  terms.
- Its people use bone piercings and tattoos and retain a deliberately rough,
  bandit-like presentation.
- They value the unusual bones extracted from the village's bone quarry.
- They have settled down without necessarily adopting the village's norms or
  becoming respectable in outsiders' eyes.

## Tentative ideas

- Camp members may practice dispersed farming, foraging, or both.
- They may trade plant foods and medicines that grow in the wilderness and are
  difficult for villagers to obtain.

These possibilities can help explain the material value of the relationship,
but they are not yet established.

## Thematic relevance

The camp complicates a simple division between lawful community and disposable
enemy. Its people have a history of imposing costs on vulnerable travelers, but
the village found a mutually useful arrangement that did not require conquest,
extermination, imprisonment, or total assimilation.

That makes the relationship a potential example of cultivation and autonomy
without erasing the camp's past predation. The demo conflict may pressure this
arrangement, but that has not yet been selected.

## Open questions

- What does the group call itself, and why did its members first gather here?
- Why did they rob travelers, and why was trade preferable?
- What do they receive from the village besides quarry bones?
- Why are the bones valuable to them?
- Is the truce personal, customary, economic, or formally secured through
  chess?
- How do individual villagers and camp members feel about one another?
- Do travelers, tournament authorities, or the monarchy recognize the change,
  or still classify them as bandits?
- What internal authority or decision-making system does the camp use?
- What would make them return to banditry?
