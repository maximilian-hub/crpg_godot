# Core theme

Status: Working

## Confirmed central question

> When the world and its institutions treat people as disposable pawns, how do
> you live a good life without becoming a piece on their board?

This is the primary organizing question for the world, player journey, major
characters, location conflicts, quests, choices, and endings.

The question contains two related dangers:

- **Being made a piece:** surrendering agency or being expended for someone
  else's design.
- **Becoming a player of people:** securing one's own agency and elevation by
  treating others as pieces.

“A good life” is deliberately contested. Characters and communities can value
freedom, belonging, duty, care, achievement, security, pleasure, legacy, or
transcendence differently.

## Primary spectrum of answers

| Position | Characteristic answer | Direction of sacrifice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cultivation | “I sacrifice myself to elevate others. Everyone sacrifices; everyone is elevated.” | Costs are accepted or shared so that people can grow together. |
| Opportunistic middle | “The system is rigged anyway, so I might as well get mine.” | Costs are avoided, redirected, or tolerated as long as one's own life improves. |
| Domination | “I sacrifice others to elevate myself. The road to our Kingdom will be drenched in the blood of the weak.” | Costs are imposed downward to accumulate power, security, or transcendence above. |

The spectrum is diagnostic, not a morality meter. Cultivation can become
martyrdom, coercive benevolence, or an expectation that vulnerable people always
give more. A self-described pragmatist may still nurture a few people fiercely.
A dominator may sincerely believe only concentrated power can protect anyone.
The writing should test each answer's behavior and cost rather than accepting a
character's self-description.

## Dimensions within an answer

Characters who occupy a similar position can differ through questions such as:

- Who do they recognize as fully deserving of a good life?
- Who chooses a sacrifice, and who is merely told it is necessary?
- Who bears the cost and who receives the benefit?
- Do they pursue reform, mutual aid, escape, withdrawal, capture, destruction,
  obedience, or control?
- What would they never sacrifice?
- How does their answer change when they are afraid, powerful, loved, or
  desperate?
- Is their private behavior consistent with their public philosophy?

These dimensions can later become secondary thematic spectra if the wider story
repeatedly organizes around them.

## Use in characters

A major character should embody an answer through choices, relationships, and
material behavior—not merely state a thesis. Their page should record:

- The answer they profess
- The answer they actually live
- The experience that made the answer persuasive
- The good their answer makes possible
- The harm or blind spot it produces
- The pressure that could strengthen, complicate, or overturn it

## Use in locations and quests

Each major location conflict should pose a specific variation of the question.
For example, it may ask who should bear the cost of prosperity, whether safety
justifies control, or whether withdrawal from a corrupt institution protects a
community or abandons others to it.

Quests then turn those positions into action. Choices should let the player
enact an answer, while consequences show who was elevated, who was diminished,
and whether the player preserved agency without taking it from someone else.

## Use in the player journey

The surface Champion track lets the player pursue mastery and a good life
inside the world's most visible institution without requiring them to diagnose
the entire system.

The subterranean occult track increasingly reveals people being treated as
literal resources in a design for institutional and supernatural hegemony. Its
endgame offers the player a defining answer: resist the machinery of domination
or seize it and attempt to become master of the board.

The [story overview](../story/overview.md) describes both tracks.

## First playable test

The [opening tavern challenge](../quests/opening-tavern-challenge.md) compresses
the question to one room. A powerful traveler humiliates a frightened child
while the crowd remains silent. The player can accept likely harm to intervene,
avoid the cost, or affiliate with the bully. Intervention cultivates the boy's
future even if the player loses; complicity offers future access to the
bully's power while ending that future in chess.

## Emergent themes not yet formalized

Existing material may eventually support additional questions around:

- Power versus withdrawal
- Mastery versus uncertainty
- Rules versus justice
- Victory versus stewardship

These are motifs and candidates, not adopted secondary themes yet.
