# Tavernkeeper

Status: Seed

“Tavernkeeper” is a development identifier. Her name is open.

## Role

- **One-line identity:** A bright, practical young woman running the
  [starting village](../locations/starting-village.md) tavern while caring for
  her sick elderly father.
- **Story function:** First established member of the player's first real
  community; she rescues the player and frames the first thematic choice.
- **Primary location:** The village tavern

## Established foundation

- Her father previously ran the tavern.
- He is now elderly and sick, and she cares for him while running the business.
- He was a highly respected voice in village affairs and a leader among the
  elders.
- Their family is probably among the village's more prosperous because the
  tavern benefits from traveler traffic.
- She nevertheless presents as ordinary and working-class rather than wealthy
  or elevated above her neighbors.
- She wears practical clothes, keeps her hair loosely tied up, and is usually
  occupied with work.
- She is bright, friendly, straightforward, and maternal without being
  saccharine.

## Opening behavior

After character creation, she finds the player collapsed outside the tavern at
night in the rain. She brings the stranger inside and puts them up in a room
before knowing what they can offer her.

Late the following day, she approaches the player nervously during the
[tavern challenge](../quests/opening-tavern-challenge.md). She explains that the
brute has been bullying everyone with a powerful king and expresses concern for
both the boy and her business.

- If the player stands up to the brute, she lets them continue staying for free,
  reasoning that they are good for business.
- If the player ignores or joins the bullying, she charges them for continued
  room and board.

## Father's final day

During the later bridge deliberation, residents ask whether her father is well
enough to advise them. She reports that he had a bad morning but reluctantly
agrees to a visit after people continue pressing.

He dies lucidly after saying farewell to her and the community. The player is
the only person who hears his final opposition to selling the road clearly. Her
response to the player's report, and to any later discovery of a lie, remains to
be designed.

See [tavernkeeper's father](tavernkeeper-father.md) and the
[bridge decision](../quests/bridge-decision.md).

## Current characterization

- **First impression:** Capable, busy, welcoming, and easy to speak with
- **Voice:** Direct and warm; no ornamental politeness or exaggerated folksiness
- **Social position:** Materially comfortable by local standards but embedded
  in daily labor and community life
- **Contradiction to develop:** Her family has prosperity and inherited social
  influence, while her presentation and work remain ordinary

Her warmth should be expressed through attention, competence, candor, and
practical care rather than generic sweetness.

## Thematic opportunity, not yet an answer

Caregiving and her unprompted rescue of the player place cultivation close to
her life, but she does not directly confront the dangerous brute. She instead
turns to another stranger while balancing concern for a child, her patrons, and
the survival of her business. This establishes generous instincts with practical
limits rather than a pure ideological position.

The writing has not decided whether caring for her father feels like chosen
cultivation, family love, inherited duty, lost opportunity, unfair expectation,
or several at once. Her father and the village may also rely on her in different
ways.

That tension can connect her to the core question without predetermining her
position. She should be developed after the village conflict clarifies what she
wants beyond meeting everyone else's needs.

## Open questions

- What is her name and age?
- What does she want for herself?
- How does she feel about taking over the tavern and caring for her father?
- What is her father's illness, and what can he still do or communicate?
- Has his respected civic role informally passed to her, and does she want it?
- How does relative prosperity affect her relationships in the village?
- What does she think of the canyon camp and its trade relationship?
- Does constant contact with travelers make her curious about leaving, glad to
  remain, or both?
- How skilled is she at chess, and what does playing reveal that tavern work
  does not?
- What prevents her or the onlookers from confronting the brute directly?
- Does she explicitly ask the player to intervene, or only explain what is
  happening and let them decide?
- How does grief affect her ability or desire to participate in the bridge
  decision?
- What would make her believe or doubt the player's report of her father's last
  words?
- Which answer to the village's thematic conflict does she embody, and what is
  the cost of that answer?
