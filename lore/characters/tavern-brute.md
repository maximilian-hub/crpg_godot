# Tavern brute

Status: Seed

“Tavern brute” is a development identifier. His name and species are open.

## Role

- **One-line identity:** A large, somewhat drunk, orc-like traveler who uses a
  powerful king and the threat behind it to dominate the starting tavern.
- **Story function:** The player's first opportunity to oppose, ignore, or
  affiliate with someone exercising power through humiliation.
- **Primary quest:** [Opening tavern challenge](../quests/opening-tavern-challenge.md)

## Established opening behavior

- He is not a local.
- He is physically imposing, loud, and somewhat drunk.
- He has built up a winning streak against people in the tavern.
- His king is powerful enough that nobody present wants to stand up to him.
- He enjoys the discomfort of a timid young opponent and publicly boasts about
  his success.
- When the boy makes a clever disruptive move, he slams his fists into the
  table, berates him, and escalates when the boy cries.
- He challenges the player if they intervene.
- He holds a personal grudge if the player stands against him, regardless of
  whether they win.
- If the player ignores or joins his cruelty, he regards them favorably and
  later shares power and resources.

## Thematic position

His opening behavior embodies domination: he converts superior physical and
chess power into status, amusement, and control while making a child bear the
cost.

That describes his behavior in this scene, not his complete character. His
history, values, loyalties, capacity for affection, and understanding of power
must give him dimensions beyond being a tutorial bully—especially if friendship
with him becomes a meaningful long-term path.

## Open questions

- What is his name, species, and place of origin?
- Why is he traveling through the village?
- Which king does he use, and what emotional landscape connects him to it?
- Is his drunkenness habitual, situational, performative, or incidental?
- Why does he value a player who laughs or stands aside?
- What power and resources can he later share?
- Does he intend to physically harm the boy or the player through a final blow?
- What does he respect besides strength or complicity?
- Can his relationship with the player develop beyond grudge versus favor?
- What good, if any, does his worldview make possible for people he considers
  his own?
