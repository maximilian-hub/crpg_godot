# Tavernkeeper's father

Status: Seed

“Tavernkeeper's father” is a development identifier. His name is open.

## Role

- **One-line identity:** The village's sick former tavernkeeper and most capable
  elder, whose absence is only fully felt when the bridge crisis arrives.
- **Primary location:** [Starting village tavern](../locations/starting-village.md)
- **Primary storyline:** [Bridge decision](../quests/bridge-decision.md)

## Established foundation

- He previously ran the village tavern.
- He was a highly respected voice and leader among the elders.
- He is now elderly, seriously ill, and cared for by his daughter, the current
  [tavernkeeper](tavernkeeper.md).
- He adamantly opposed E4's earlier proposal to acquire the village road and
  build an interstate.
- The village managed his decline without a leadership crisis while ordinary
  life remained stable.
- The damaged bridge produces the first problem large enough to expose the loss
  of his coordinating influence.

## Final scene

During the bridge deliberation, the remaining elders ask whether he is well
enough to speak. His daughter says he had a bad morning but reluctantly agrees
after people continue pressing.

He is dying but lucid when they visit. He is happy to see his daughter, friends,
and neighbors and is more interested in affectionate memories, farewells, and
well wishes than in running one last practical meeting.

When someone finally presses him about selling to E4 or repairing the bridge,
his last smiling words are approximately:

> Don't sell, you fools. [Village name] can fix it.

Only the player hears him clearly. His words become the subject of the player's
first major act of testimony: report them honestly or claim he supported the
sale.

## Thematic position

He believes the village is capable of directing and sustaining itself. That
faith can inspire collective cultivation, but it should not automatically make
every practical implication correct. Repair still exposes real people to danger
and cost.

His final wish should be treated as the conviction of a beloved person, not a
dead patriarch's unquestionable command. The living community remains
responsible for deciding what his values require under present conditions.

## Open questions

- What is his name, age, and illness?
- What made him the elders' most capable coordinator?
- Why did he oppose E4 so strongly the first time?
- Did he reject only the specific terms, or corporate control in principle?
- What was his relationship with the canyon camp and its former banditry?
- What did he teach his daughter, and where does she disagree with him?
- Does he know who the player is or what they did during the tavern challenge?
- Why is the player physically close enough to hear his final words?
- Who pressures him for a practical answer during his farewell?
