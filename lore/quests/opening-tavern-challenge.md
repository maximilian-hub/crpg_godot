# Opening: tavern challenge

Status: Working

## Purpose

- **Place in the story:** First playable sequence and first consequential player
  choice after character creation
- **Major theme:** [Living a good life without becoming a piece or treating
  others as pieces](../themes/core.md)
- **Thematic question:** Will the player accept personal risk to protect a
  vulnerable stranger when everyone else remains silent, avoid the cost, or
  seek favor by participating in his humiliation?
- **Primary location:** [Starting village tavern](../locations/starting-village.md)

This is a small-scale statement of the entire game's question. Intervention is
meaningful whether the player wins or loses; what matters first is the choice to
accept danger rather than the power to guarantee victory.

## Arrival

1. Character creation ends.
2. At night in heavy rain, the player character walks slowly into view from the
   right and collapses outside the tavern.
3. The [tavernkeeper](../characters/tavernkeeper.md) finds them, brings them
   inside, and gives them a room.
4. Perspective resumes late the following day as the player heads downstairs
   into the common room.

The tavernkeeper therefore acts toward the player before knowing whether they
can repay or benefit her. Her care establishes the behavior against which the
player's coming choice can be read.

## The confrontation

A small crowd of colorful travelers and locals surrounds a chess table.

- One player is the [tavern brute](../characters/tavern-brute.md), a large,
  somewhat drunk, orc-like outsider with a powerful king and a loud winning
  streak.
- His opponent is a [timid human boy](../characters/tavern-boy.md), no older
  than twelve and visibly shaken.
- The brute enjoys the boy's discomfort and boasts to the room.
- The tavernkeeper nervously explains that he has been bullying everyone with
  his king. She is worried about the child and about the effect on her business.
- The boy makes a clever move that disrupts the brute's strategy.
- The brute slams his fists into the table, berates the boy, and continues when
  the boy begins to cry.
- The room goes quiet. No onlooker intervenes.

## Player responses

### Stand up to the brute

The brute challenges the player. His powerful king makes this an overwhelmingly
difficult first game, and the expected result is a loss, but victory remains a
real outcome and must be acknowledged.

Whether the player wins or loses, their intervention ends the immediate abuse.
The match result and the decision to intervene are separate pieces of story
state.

### Ignore the situation

The player declines the personal cost and allows the scene to continue. This is
not identical in intent to joining the cruelty and should remain distinguishable
in persistent state even where their largest consequences overlap.

### Laugh with the brute

The player participates in the boy's humiliation and seeks affinity with the
person currently holding power.

## Persistent consequences

| Consequence | Stand up | Ignore or join |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tavernkeeper | Gives the player continued room and board for free; their presence is good for the tavern | Charges the player for continued room and board |
| Boy | Is inspired to become a great player and eventually aids the player later | Is traumatized and puts down his chess pieces forever |
| Brute | Begins the game with a personal grudge against the player | Has a positive attitude and later shares power and resources |
| Significant onlookers | Initial attitudes change according to what they value and what they witnessed | Initial attitudes change according to what they value and what they witnessed |

Ignoring and actively joining can share the major boy, tavern, and brute branches
while still producing different memories and reactions among characters.

The rewards should remain morally complicated. Standing up creates hospitality
and a future ally but also an enemy. Complicity creates access to a powerful
person's resources at the cost of the boy's future and the tavernkeeper's
respect.

## Structural reconvergence

After any response, the player enters a required interaction with the
[professor](../characters/professor.md). The professor plays an ordinary game of
chess with them and gives them their first king piece.

This reconverges the tutorial and Champion track without erasing what happened
in the tavern.

## Suggested story state

| State | Values |
| --- | --- |
| Opening response | `defended`, `ignored`, `joined` |
| Brute match result | `won`, `lost`, `not_played` |
| Tavern lodging | `comped`, `paid` |
| Boy's path | `inspired`, `quit_chess` |
| Brute's initial relationship | `grudge`, `favorable` |

Individual onlookers can derive an initial impression from the opening response
without requiring a single universal approval score.

## Rules and staging dependencies

- Establish what king or ordinary playing method the player uses before the
  professor grants their first king piece.
- Make the possibility of bodily harm legible before the player chooses to
  intervene.
- Decide whether the brute accepts a resignation or intentionally lands a
  harmful final strike if the player loses.
- Clarify who stops the confrontation after a loss and why the brute does not
  continue abusing the boy.
- Preserve a genuine win reaction for unusually skilled or persistent players;
  do not turn the likely loss into a forced false choice.
- Give the boy enough agency and specificity that his clever move and later
  development matter beyond functioning as a moral prop.
