CHESS RPG — CURRENT AI OPPONENTS
================================

Last reviewed: November 2026

Purpose
-------

This document describes how the selectable CPU brains currently choose actions.
It is an implementation reference, not a promise that every heuristic is final.
AI design goals and future opponent-knowledge ideas remain in
AI_DESIGN_NORTH_STAR.txt. Rules ownership and native-performance intentions live
in PURE_DATA_AUTHORITATIVE_CORE_INTENT.txt.


Shared CPU Execution
--------------------

ChessCpuPlayer owns one selected brain per army: Ryck, LeMay, or Dupre. It asks
the authoritative model for legal actions, lets the brain choose a plan, then
matches the plan back to a still-legal authoritative action before execution.
This revision check prevents an obsolete thought from acting on a changed board.

The CPU player also resolves required selections such as promotion, Raise Dead,
and Quarterly Initiative movement grants. Seeded randomness is used where a
brain intentionally chooses among equivalent options. Sandbox armies may select
brains independently, allowing matches such as Ryck versus Dupre.


Ryck — Brief Summary
--------------------

Ryck is the original fast heuristic opponent. He scores the current legal action
list without opponent-reply search, using shared tactical filters and older
king-specific behavior in ChessCpuPlayer. He is inexpensive and lively, but can
miss elementary consequences because he does not simulate the opponent's next
turn. Ryck's behavior is intentionally retained as its own selectable brain.


LeMay — Brief Summary
---------------------

LeMay is the original general two-ply minimax experiment. He simulates each root
move and the opponent's replies with authoritative ChessBoardModels, uses
alpha-beta pruning, and accepts only completed minimax candidates as exact.
LeMay has a five-second soft budget and cooperative main-thread yielding, but his
generic evaluation and exhaustive model-based work make him slower and less
purposeful than Dupre. Current enhanced king support is limited to Classic and
Arakne.


Dupre — Overview
----------------

Dupre combines three kinds of intelligence:

1. A fast traditional opening book.
2. A cheap purposeful policy that ranks every legal action.
3. Selective authoritative tactical verification of the most important moves.

This is not a conventional deep chess engine. Dupre normally looks one move
ahead and examines the opponent's immediate replies (two plies total), with a
limited extra tactical continuation at unstable leaves. His strength comes from
choosing useful candidates, recognizing obligations, ordering dangerous replies
first, and understanding Chess RPG consequences—not from searching many turns.


Dupre's Decision Pipeline
-------------------------

1. Validate support and capture the current revision

Dupre declines to think if either king lacks a registered, validated Dupre
layer. Legal actions always come from the authoritative rules. A changed model
revision invalidates a thought before it can execute.

2. Build the tactical view

DupreTacticalMap records occupancy and lazily answers attack, defense, hanging-
piece, and king-attacker questions. Projected maps reuse unchanged occupancy and
calculate only relationships actually queried for an action. This is a cheap
policy aid, not a second legality engine.

DupreExchangeAnalyzer estimates short capture/recapture sequences, material
swings, and the risk to threatened pieces. Its confidence is reduced for durable
pieces and kings, where simple orthodox exchange assumptions are less reliable.

3. Try the opening-book fast path

At the beginning of a compatible match, Dupre uniformly selects one opening
family and remains committed to it. The current generated runtime book contains
48 families and is approximately 347 KB. Its source data is imported offline
from lichess-org/chess-openings; runtime play does not parse SAN or download
data.

The default ceiling is seven successfully executed book moves by Dupre, not
seven full rounds. Recognized opponent replies advance the selected branch.
After a modest deviation, compatible recovery moves may still be offered.

Every recommendation is matched to an authoritative legal action. Dupre leaves
or overrides the book for immediate wins, direct king attacks, king danger,
valuable threatened pieces, certain ability threats, imminent scheduled
hazards, or strategically important king abilities. A normal safe book hit uses
no tactical simulations.

Opening state resets when the brain/session or position is reset. Book actions
increment the limit only after successful authoritative execution.

4. Rank all acceptable actions with purposeful policy

DupreMovePolicy begins with Ryck's established action score, then adds a more
deliberate positional and tactical interpretation. Important considerations
include:

* immediate victory and direct damage to the enemy king;
* development of knights, bishops, and useful pawn moves;
* rook activity and a penalty for purposeless back-rank rook shuffling;
* centralization of knights, bishops, and queens;
* immediate move-reversal avoidance;
* safe captures and estimated capture/recapture exchanges;
* rescuing threatened valuable pieces;
* penalties for moving onto attacked squares or exposing undefended allies;
* king safety, including survivable counterattacks and capturing an attacker;
* current and near-future enemy ability effects;
* pending hazards such as Cannon mortar impacts;
* short-cycle/repeated-position penalties; and
* endgame technique.

The endgame policy blends in as non-pawn material falls. It values passed pawns,
promotion races, king activity and escort, rooks behind passers, sensible trades
while ahead, and selected rook endgame technique. Its root-policy influence is
kept modest so it cannot overwhelm urgent tactics.

5. Preserve mandatory and strategically important candidates

Dupre does not simply search the top N policy scores. Before adding the default
six ordinary policy candidates, he preserves relevant actions from categories
such as:

* immediate wins;
* responses to king danger;
* direct king attacks;
* captures of king attackers;
* responses to threatened major pieces and ability obligations;
* responses to scheduled hazards;
* favorable captures;
* forcing or high-impact abilities; and
* king-specific strategic ability actions.

Caps prevent any one ability category from consuming the entire budget, but the
final candidate set may be larger than six because mandatory actions are added
outside the ordinary policy allowance.

6. Prepare every candidate before deepening any one candidate

Search preparation is breadth-first. Dupre simulates every retained root action
before allowing a single favorite to consume the reply budget. Opponent replies
are ordered with wins, king damage, captures, serious threats, and forcing
abilities first.

Dupre then advances reply analysis round-robin across root candidates:

* first, one forcing reply per candidate per round;
* then, remaining replies per candidate per round; and
* alpha-beta cutoffs when a candidate is already no better than a completed
  alternative.

This prevents legal-action order from deciding which move happens to receive
useful analysis before timeout.

7. Evaluate authoritative resulting states

Production Dupre normally imports the settled position once into
ClassicChessCore and simulates copied pure-data ChessCoreStates. These searches
do not instantiate temporary scene-tree nodes. The pure core owns movement,
combat, abilities, reactions, random state, hazards, and turn resolution.

The static evaluation principally considers:

* victory or defeat;
* material and partial damage to durable pieces;
* king HP, with a much larger value than ordinary material;
* mobility;
* hanging rooks and queens;
* direct king pressure and immediate lethal actions;
* granted movement types;
* supported king-specific positional state; and
* blended endgame technique.

If a position cannot be imported into the pure backend, Dupre can fall back to
authoritative ChessBoardModel simulation. Unsupported king types are normally
rejected earlier rather than guessed at.

8. Extend tactically unstable leaves

Quiescence adds one selective continuation for immediate tactical actions. It
includes captures, king damage, lethal or major material damage, consequential
forced movement, and genuinely urgent ability consequences. Quiet summons,
delayed Wraith pressure, ordinary Quarterly grants, and scheduled Cannon mortar
placements do not expand the tactical leaf merely because they are abilities.

If the budget expires during this extension, the leaf is marked incomplete. It
is never silently promoted to an exact score.

9. Select an exact result or an honest fallback

An exact candidate has completed all required opponent replies. Dupre chooses
among tactically near-equal exact candidates using the purposeful policy, which
allows chess style and activity to matter after tactical safety is established.

Incomplete minimizing scores are optimistic bounds and are never compared as
if they were exact. If no root completes, Dupre uses a classified breadth-first
fallback. Known terminal or severe losses are disfavored; complete forcing-reply
coverage, mandatory tactical purpose, policy score, and stable source order
guide the fallback. Diagnostics explicitly mark this choice as unverified.


Budgets and Main-Thread Responsiveness
--------------------------------------

Dupre currently inherits these production limits from LeMay:

* five-second soft thinking budget;
* 4,096 evaluated opponent-reply leaves; and
* approximately 3 ms cooperative simulation slices.

Policy preparation uses a somewhat wider cooperative threshold for batches of
cheap cached queries. All SceneTree interaction remains on the main thread.
Pure-data simulation removes most node construction from the hot loop, but it is
still GDScript running on the main thread; cooperative yields reduce stalls but
cannot preempt one unusually expensive authoritative operation midway through.

Opening-book decisions bypass ordinary tactical search when safe and should be
near-immediate. Presentation delays and animations are separate from AI compute.

The sandbox exposes independent Dupre limits for White and Black. Timed search
may be set from 0.5 to 20 seconds with a slider or typed value. Developers may
also disable the time limit, the reply/work cap, or both. With both disabled,
the current search still terminates because its candidate set, opponent replies,
and one-step quiescence extension are finite; cooperative yielding remains on.


King Support
------------

Dupre currently registers these king types:

* Classic — generic rules-compatible support.
* Arakne — enhanced Spike Burst threats and path-preserving Skitter simulation.
* Necromancer — enhanced summon placement, Raise Dead settlement, Bone Pawn
  consequences, and exchange adjustments.
* Minotaur — enhanced Charge and Retaliating Rage consequences.
* Quarterly Initiative — enhanced grant selection, HP-sacrifice policy, and
  movement-grant consequences.
* Cannon — enhanced mortar footprint, friendly fire, scheduled-hazard pressure,
  and reaction consequences.
* Rhetorician — enhanced Compel, forced movement/capture, Lunacy, and Socratic
  consequences.
* Wraith — enhanced summon targeting, travel pressure, replacement cost,
  projectiles, and Cursed Mark consequences.
* Archer variants — generic rules-compatible support only. Bespoke strategy is
  intentionally deferred while the final Archer design is unsettled. Registered
  IDs are archer_king, archer_king_draw, archer_king_quiver,
  archer_king_combination, and archer_king_charge.

The authoritative core defines what these abilities actually do. AI layers add
strategic descriptions, threat interpretation, move ordering, and policy—not a
duplicate executable rules engine.


State, Randomness, and Reproducibility
--------------------------------------

Dupre retains limited match state:

* selected opening family and branch;
* successfully played book moves;
* previous own actions for reversal detection; and
* settled-position history for repetition detection.

The CPU debug context captures the brain, color, RNG state, safety state, and
brain session state. Game recordings may also contain decision telemetry and
candidate diagnostics. Pure simulations copy authoritative state, including RNG
state, so speculative branches do not advance the live game's random stream.


Diagnostics
-----------

Each Dupre thought exposes a search profile and a bounded candidate report.
Useful fields include total thinking time, simulation backend, book hit/fallback
reason, policy and candidate-selection time, legal-action generations, core
resolutions, replies available and evaluated, forcing coverage, exact/pruned/
incomplete/invalid roots, quiescence nodes, alpha-beta cutoffs, fallback risk,
repetition evidence, cooperative yields, and maximum uninterrupted computation.

These diagnostics are the main reason annotated representative .crpggame files
are useful: two turns that both consume the five-second ceiling may nevertheless
perform very different amounts and kinds of work.


Important Current Limitations
-----------------------------

* Dupre does not plan several quiet moves ahead. Long-term plans are approximated
  by policy, opening theory, endgame technique, and immediate-reply search.
* The five-second budget means busy positions may end with an explicitly
  unverified fallback, even after dangerous replies were screened fairly.
* Tactical maps and exchange sequences are selective approximations. Final
  legality and simulated consequences always come from the authoritative core.
* Archer variants have legality support but no finalized bespoke strategy.
* Opponent king knowledge is currently perfect for every supported king. The
  Unknown/Observed/Familiar design in AI_DESIGN_NORTH_STAR.txt is not yet active.
* Character personalities, opening preferences, risk tolerance, and theatrical
  thinking delays are not yet implemented as opponent-specific traits.
* The pure authoritative core is optimized GDScript, not a native module. Native
  experimentation remains a possible later optimization after profiling.
