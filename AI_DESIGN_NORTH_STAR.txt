CHESS RPG · AI DESIGN NORTH STAR

A world of chess players, not chess engines
-The opponent should feel like a person with established chess knowledge, recognizable habits, imperfect judgment, and an individual playing style.

Opening familiarity
-Opening moves are selected quickly and confidently from a broad repertoire of recognizable traditional chess lines, with future Chess RPG–specific theory mixed in.

Separation of thinking and acting
-The AI computes as quickly as possible. A separate character-direction system controls hesitation, confidence, physical animation, and dramatic pacing.

Flexible opening knowledge
-Books remain useful across unusual kings and minor board deviations. A configurable move limit ends book use, and king-specific behavior may override it.

Distinct personalities
-Characters eventually will express different strategic preferences, piece affinities, risk tolerances, and playing strengths through reusable parameters.

Future-proof mechanics
-Future abilities, hazards, baubles(planned gem-slot items that can slightly modify a king piece), and future player skills should enter through shared game-rule interfaces rather than requiring redesigns of the opponent AI.



OPENING NORTH STAR
  Familiar chess, confidently played
  Dupre should feel like an experienced resident of a world where chess is common knowledge. He begins games with a broad repertoire of recognizable chess openings, playing familiar continuations immediately and confidently.
  He commits to an opening family, adapts when opponents deviate, and tolerates unusual Chess RPG mechanics without instantly forgetting his opening knowledge.
  His knowledge is imperfect: an opening may be strategically questionable against a particular king. He is not required to play optimally, but he should still respond to obvious immediate danger.
  After a configurable number of his own book moves, he transitions to his normal decision-making system.
  The AI selects moves as quickly as possible. Character direction—not AI search—controls physical hesitation, animation, and dramatic timing.
  Dupre should capable of having this opening behaviour overridden, like if he's using a king that wants to use its ability on Turn 1.

  The concrete design decisions
  Aspect:	V1 decision
  Opening repertoire:	Many recognizable traditional openings
  Selection	Uniform: random choice among eligible opening families
  Commitment:	Choose one family per match
  Length:	Configurable maximum number of Dupre's book moves
  Opponent: deviations	Try compatible continuations, then normal Dupre
  Minor RPG actions:	Do not automatically invalidate opening context
  Tactical emergencies:	Override book preferences
  King-specific rules:	May override or disable book recommendations
  Book performance:	Fast-path decisions, not full search by default
  Character timing:	Controlled entirely outside the AI
  Future personalities:	Supported by extensibility points; not built yet
  Future Chess RPG theory:	Can coexist with traditional opening knowledge
  
  One nuance I'd include: the maximum book length is a ceiling, not a promise. Dupre might leave his book on move two because the position demands it, or continue until move six if everything remains familiar.
  And selecting an opening family should not mean predetermining an exact move sequence regardless of the opponent's choices. The family can contain multiple branches.
