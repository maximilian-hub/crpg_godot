CHESS RPG — AI DESIGN NORTH STAR
================================

Purpose
-------

This document defines how an opponent should feel. Technical rules ownership
and the performance roadmap live in PURE_DATA_AUTHORITATIVE_CORE_INTENT.txt.


A World of Chess Players, Not Chess Engines
-------------------------------------------

An opponent should feel like a person with established chess knowledge,
recognizable habits, imperfect judgment, and an individual playing style.
Playing strength matters, but purposeful and legible behavior matters too.

The AI computes as quickly as possible. Character direction—not search—controls
hesitation, confidence, physical animation, and dramatic timing.


Dupre's Playing Model
---------------------

Dupre combines three kinds of intelligence:

* Opening knowledge for quick, recognizable traditional play.
* A purposeful policy for development, activity, king safety, material safety,
  repetition avoidance, and sensible use of king abilities.
* Selective authoritative tactical search for immediate wins, defenses,
  captures, king damage, forced movement, and costly blunders.

Tactical uncertainty must remain explicit. An incompletely verified move is not
silently treated as proven safe.


Opening Principles
------------------

* Choose one compatible opening family per match rather than rerolling each turn.
* Select eligible families uniformly unless a future personality says otherwise.
* Follow branches in response to the opponent; do not predetermine a line.
* Treat the configured book length as a ceiling, not a promise.
* Permit compatible continuations after modest deviations or unrelated RPG
  actions, but leave the book when the position no longer supports the theory.
* Let immediate wins, urgent defenses, and king-specific priorities override
  book recommendations.
* Keep book decisions fast. Presentation timing belongs outside the AI.
* Allow traditional theory to remain available even when an unusual RPG king
  makes it strategically imperfect.


Scaling to Many Kings
---------------------

The authoritative core defines what every action does. Generic AI policy should
understand common consequences—damage, captures, summons, displacement, stun,
delayed hazards, protection, and king danger—without bespoke matchup code.

King-specific AI extensions are appropriate for strategic intent that cannot be
inferred from the generic consequences. They should remain optional additions,
not a requirement to reimplement each king's rules for every opponent.

Future abilities, hazards, equipment, baubles, and player skills should enter
through shared action/state interfaces. A roster of more than one hundred kings
must not require a separate handcrafted evaluator for every pairing.


Opponent Knowledge and Discovery
--------------------------------

NPC opponents should not automatically possess perfect knowledge of every king.
In the setting, most players specialize in one king or a small collection. An
ordinary farmer, butcher, or goblin should not immediately understand an obscure
king recovered from a distant ruin merely because the authoritative simulator
knows its rules.

Future AI should separate the true rules from the opponent's belief about those
rules. A useful knowledge progression is:

* Unknown: anticipate ordinary Classic-like behavior and visible universal game
  information, but not undisclosed special mechanics.
* Observed: recognize demonstrated mechanics well enough to avoid repeating an
  immediately obvious mistake, without gaining complete tactical mastery.
* Familiar: include the king's full known capabilities in tactical forecasting
  and strategic counterplay.

Observed knowledge is deliberately weaker than Familiar knowledge. Seeing a
Minotaur Charge or Retaliating Rage once may teach an NPC not to feed another
piece into the same obvious consequence. It should not instantly grant the move
ordering, setup recognition, timing, and counterplay of someone who has studied
or reflected on that king. Familiarity may be awarded between matches, allowing
an NPC to become meaningfully more effective in a rematch.

This distinction supports both immersion and gameplay: an unfamiliar king gives
the player a temporary discovery advantage, while repeated opponents learn and
encourage the player to rotate kings.

The learning policy must remain configurable. Content or game modes must be able
to disable mid-match observation entirely, prevent knowledge persistence,
promote directly from Unknown to Familiar, or assign starting familiarity from
an NPC's lore and experience. A simple initial implementation may use only
Unknown and Familiar, but it must not make the intermediate Observed state
architecturally mandatory.

Ignorance affects prediction, never legality or perception. An NPC still obeys
the authoritative rules, understands its own king, sees current HP and visible
board state, and responds to consequences that have already occurred. Knowledge
filters belong in AI policy, threat interpretation, and opponent-reply modeling;
they must never modify the authoritative core position.

Persisted knowledge should eventually be keyed by NPC identity and opposing king
type, independently from the player's KingDex. The KingDex represents player
discovery; NPC knowledge represents that character's personal experience.


Future Personalities
--------------------

Characters may eventually vary through reusable preferences: opening-family
weights, aggression, risk tolerance, piece affinity, ability appetite,
repetition tolerance, tactical depth, and willingness to accept uncertainty.

Personality should shape choices among reasonable moves. It should not require
characters to ignore immediate victory, obvious catastrophic danger, or the
authoritative rules of the game.
