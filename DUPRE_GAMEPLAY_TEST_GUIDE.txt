DUPRE GAMEPLAY TEST GUIDE
=========================

Why Save Games?
---------------

Saved games let us replay the exact position where Dupre made an interesting,
foolish, or impressive decision. The strongest examples can become permanent
automated tests, helping us improve Dupre's judgment without breaking behavior
that already works.

Human playtesting also catches something mechanical tests cannot: moves that
are technically legal and safe, but feel pointless, unnatural, frustrating, or
unfun. A small number of clearly explained examples is more useful than a large
pile of unexplained game files.


What Games to Save
------------------

Please save a game when Dupre:

* Gives away material or overlooks an obvious capture.
* Misses an immediate attack, defense, victory, or king-damage opportunity.
* Uses a king ability at a strange time or misunderstands another king's ability.
* Repeats moves, shuffles pieces, or appears to have no useful plan.
* Handles an opening, promotion race, rook ending, or other endgame poorly.
* Makes a move that is safe but feels conspicuously passive or purposeless.
* Makes an unusually clever, convincing, or enjoyable move we should preserve.

Games Dupre wins are useful too. Please include both colors, different king
matchups, and games that reach an endgame when convenient. There is no quota:
one especially clear example may be more valuable than ten ordinary games.

Ordinary games are also useful for performance work. New recordings retain a
small per-turn summary of how much search Dupre completed inside his fixed time
budget, where that work was spent, whether he fell back to an unverified move,
and his longest uninterrupted compute interval. You do not need to recognize or
annotate technical slowdowns; a varied collection supplies the comparison data.


What Notes to Leave
-------------------

In the sandbox, navigate to the interesting settled turn and choose Flag Frame.
Use Report Issue to attach the notes below directly to the exported game. The
report screen automatically includes the exact frame, round, matchup, AI brain,
seed, and recorded action.

Fill in the report fields as follows:

* Type: Choose the closest category—Tactical mistake, Strategic mistake,
  Ability use, Repetition / no plan, Endgame, Good play, or Other.
* Urgency: Choose Immediate / tactical if the consequence was already present
  or could happen on the next move. Choose Slower / strategic for plans,
  development, positioning, repetition, and longer-term judgment.
* What happened? Describe what Dupre did and the important pieces or threat.
  This is the only required written field.
* What did you expect? Describe one or more moves or ideas Dupre should have
  considered instead. It is fine to be uncertain about the single best move.
* Why was it interesting? Explain the consequence: lost material, missed king
  damage, misunderstood ability, pointless repetition, especially good play,
  and so forth.
* Additional notes: Add any context not obvious from the recorded position,
  such as a repeated pattern across games or something noticed during play.

You do not need to type the filename, round, color, king matchup, brain, seed,
or chosen action into these fields. The exported report supplies that context
automatically.

Example:

    Type: Tactical mistake
    Urgency: Immediate / tactical

    What happened? *
    Dupre summoned a Bone Pawn to defend his threatened queen instead of
    removing the vulnerable pawn attacking it.

    What did you expect?
    Capture the attacking pawn with the queen, or move the queen somewhere safe.

    Why was it interesting?
    The pawn took the queen and the Bone Pawn recaptured it, so Dupre effectively
    traded his queen for a pawn despite having simple alternatives.

    Additional notes
    I have seen Necromancer Dupre make this kind of defensive summon in more
    than one game.

Short notes are completely fine. Do not alter the position to make it cleaner—
the original game record is the most useful evidence.

Use Save Game or Share Game when you simply want to exchange an interesting
match for fun. Both deliberately export a clean recording without a flagged
turn or issue report. On Android, Save uses the system document picker and
Share sends a real .crpggame attachment through the share sheet. Imported games
can be watched continuously, paused after the current turn finishes animating,
or stepped backward and forward by turn.


Suggested Collection Mix
------------------------

As a loose starting target, gather about 8–12 interesting games across:

* Classic and special-king matchups.
* Dupre playing White and Black.
* Wins and losses.
* Openings, middlegames, and at least a couple of endgames.

Send games whenever they are ready. There is no need to wait for a complete
batch before reviewing a particularly clear example.
