DUPRE GAMEPLAY TEST GUIDE
=========================

Why Save Games?
---------------

Saved games let us replay the exact position where Dupre made an interesting,
foolish, or impressive decision. The strongest examples can become permanent
automated tests, helping us improve Dupre's judgment without breaking behavior
that already works.

Human playtesting also catches something mechanical tests cannot: moves that
are technically legal and safe, but feel pointless, unnatural, frustrating, or
unfun. A small number of clearly explained examples is more useful than a large
pile of unexplained game files.


What Games to Save
------------------

Please save a game when Dupre:

* Gives away material or overlooks an obvious capture.
* Misses an immediate attack, defense, victory, or king-damage opportunity.
* Uses a king ability at a strange time or misunderstands another king's ability.
* Repeats moves, shuffles pieces, or appears to have no useful plan.
* Handles an opening, promotion race, rook ending, or other endgame poorly.
* Makes a move that is safe but feels conspicuously passive or purposeless.
* Makes an unusually clever, convincing, or enjoyable move we should preserve.

Games Dupre wins are useful too. Please include both colors, different king
matchups, and games that reach an endgame when convenient. There is no quota:
one especially clear example may be more valuable than ten ordinary games.


What Notes to Leave
-------------------

In the sandbox, navigate to the interesting settled turn and choose Flag Frame.
Use Report Issue to attach the notes below directly to the exported game. The
report screen automatically includes the exact frame, round, matchup, AI brain,
seed, and recorded action.

For each reported game, record:

* The filename.
* The approximate round or turn.
* Which color and king Dupre controlled.
* What Dupre did.
* What you expected Dupre to consider instead.
* Why the move seemed bad, good, or interesting.
* Whether the problem felt urgent and tactical or slower and strategic.

Example:

    game12.crpggame
    Round 18 — Dupre was the Black Necromancer.
    Dupre summoned a Bone Pawn to defend his threatened queen.
    I expected him to capture the attacking pawn or move the queen.
    The pawn took the queen and the Bone Pawn recaptured it, so Dupre traded
    his queen for a pawn. This looked like an urgent tactical mistake.

Short notes are completely fine. If the exact round is unknown, describe the
position or say roughly when it happened. Do not alter the position to make it
cleaner—the original game record is the most useful evidence.

Use Share Game instead when you simply want to exchange an interesting match
for fun. Share Game deliberately exports a clean recording without a flagged
turn or issue report. Imported games can be watched continuously, paused after
the current turn finishes animating, or stepped backward and forward by turn.


Suggested Collection Mix
------------------------

As a loose starting target, gather about 8–12 interesting games across:

* Classic and special-king matchups.
* Dupre playing White and Black.
* Wins and losses.
* Openings, middlegames, and at least a couple of endgames.

Send games whenever they are ready. There is no need to wait for a complete
batch before reviewing a particularly clear example.
